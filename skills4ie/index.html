Skills4IE’27

Future-Proofing the Intelligent Environments Workforce
Skills, learning and credible assessment in the age of generative AI.

Conference: 21–24 June 2027 Location: Skopje, North Macedonia Format: Hybrid
Stone Bridge over the Vardar River, Skopje. Public-domain photograph via Wikimedia Commons.

Scope

What should future Intelligent Environments professionals be able to do when AI can already explain, generate, code, analyse and assist with increasingly sophisticated technical work?

Advances in artificial intelligence, cyber-physical systems, IoT, edge computing and digital twins are transforming intelligent environments and the skills needed to develop them. At the same time, AI is changing how future professionals learn, work and demonstrate expertise.

Skills4IE’27 connects three perspectives: the technical and transversal competencies required by future professionals; approaches to learning in computing and engineering education; and credible assessment in the age of generative AI. It brings together researchers, educators and industry practitioners to examine systems reasoning, human–AI collaboration, critical evaluation, verification and responsible decision-making, and to identify practical directions for curricula, learning and assessment.

SkillsWhat expertise and judgement will future professionals need?
LearningWhat educational experiences can develop those capabilities?
AssessmentWhat evidence can credibly demonstrate competence?
The workshop is built around alignment: what we want graduates to be able to do, how we help them learn it, and how we know they can actually do it.

Main topics

We welcome contributions related to the future workforce for Intelligent Environments, including but not limited to:

  • Future technical and transversal competencies for Intelligent Environments
  • Systems thinking, problem formulation and architectural reasoning
  • Human–AI collaboration in professional and educational practice
  • Critical evaluation, verification and responsible use of AI-generated outputs
  • Curriculum redesign in computing and engineering
  • Authentic, challenge-based, project-based and problem-based learning
  • Intelligent learning environments, simulations, laboratories and digital twins for learning
  • Assessment of reasoning, transfer, design judgement and decision-making
  • Assessment with and without AI
  • Process-based, oral, interactive and performance-based assessment
  • Academic integrity beyond simple AI detection
  • Industry perspectives on changing professional roles and workforce needs
  • Programme-level and institutional educational transformation
  • Empirical studies, experience reports and lessons from successful or unsuccessful interventions

Submissions

Skills4IE’27 welcomes both mature research and focused contributions that stimulate discussion and help build a shared agenda for education and workforce development in Intelligent Environments.

Full papers

For mature research contributions, empirical studies, substantial educational designs, frameworks or evaluations.

Short papers

For emerging research, work in progress, experience reports, industry perspectives, position papers and concise case studies.

Peer review and publication Accepted papers intended for publication must be peer reviewed by at least two reviewers. IE’27 states that accepted workshop papers will be published in the IOS Press Ambient Intelligence and Smart Environments book series and electronically through the ACM Digital Library. Publication remains subject to the final conference publication arrangements.

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for IE’27 at the standard registration rate and present the paper. IE’27 is hybrid; in-person attendance is preferred, while online participation may also be considered.

The exact Skills4IE paper format, page limits and submission link will be posted here once the workshop submission setup is finalised.

Workshop format

Skills4IE’27 is designed as an interactive research workshop rather than a sequence of isolated presentations. The programme will combine selected paper presentations with structured discussion and collaborative activities.

We plan to use the contributions to connect three questions:

  • What should we teach?
  • How should students learn it?
  • What evidence would convince us that they can actually do it?

IE’27 schedules workshops in parallel during the first two days of the conference. A half-day format is suggested by the conference, with the possibility of a full day depending on interest and programme constraints. The final Skills4IE’27 schedule will be announced later.
